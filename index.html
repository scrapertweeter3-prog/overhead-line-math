The overhead on a hire outprices the subscription

A senior engineer costs $18,290 a month fully loaded, $219,480 a year once the loads sit on top: benefits at 35 percent, payroll taxes at 15 percent, bonus at 15 percent, retirement at 8 percent. Back the 73 percent of add-ons out and the base salary is about $126,900. The remaining $92,610 a year buys the employment wrapper, $44,400 of benefits, $19,030 of taxes, $19,030 of bonus, $10,150 of retirement, and none of it writes code.

The subscription skips the wrapper. The yearly plan is $4,995 a month, $59,940 a year, and the entire bill is the work itself: no benefits package, no payroll tax line, no bonus pool, no $13,500 recruiting bill at the front. The non-salary overhead on one hire, $92,610, exceeds the whole plan by $32,670. Take the bonus out of the comparison entirely and benefits, taxes, and retirement still run $73,580, $13,640 over the plan.

The $13,500 upfront alone covers 2.7 months of the yearly plan. Inside the subscription the flow stays plain: one active request on a Trello board, about 24 hours to onboard, 72 hour turnaround per card, unlimited revisions, cancel anytime with the private repository kept.

Pricing and workflow sit at Poket Dev, an unlimited software development subscription where the bill is the work.
